Base Case Scenario
The Middle East conflict remains active but contained. Iranian proxies are engaged across multiple theatres; no direct US military confrontation has materialised. Energy routes through the Strait of Hormuz remain operational, with shipping costs elevated but manageable. The conflict contributes to moderate inflationary pressure on energy-sensitive insurance lines but has not triggered systemic disruption.

In Eastern Europe, the Russia-Ukraine conflict is in a prolonged stalemate. Active negotiation channels exist but no ceasefire is in place. Neither side has the capacity for a decisive offensive. Sovereign credit stress in CEE countries is elevated but contained; European energy dependency has reduced but structural vulnerability remains.

US foreign policy is marked by selective engagement and mounting fiscal constraints. The new Fed leadership raises questions on monetary policy independence, with bear steepening risk present but not materialised. Tariffs on EU goods stand at approximately 25%, with an uncoordinated EU response. Geopolitical fragmentation is advancing gradually across financial, technology, and trade dimensions — not in a disruptive discontinuity, but through accumulating structural shifts.
Worst Case Scenario
The Middle East conflict escalates into direct and sustained US military engagement. The scale of this engagement absorbs a significant share of US strategic resources, materially reducing its effective presence and credibility in other theatres. Energy supply through the Strait of Hormuz is severely disrupted for an extended period, triggering a commodity shock that amplifies inflationary pressure across all insurance lines and constrains Fed flexibility.

The reduction in US strategic bandwidth is read by Russia as a window of opportunity in Eastern Europe. The conflict expands in scale or geography, triggering wider European sovereign stress, accelerated defence spending, and deterioration in CEE credit markets. European governance fragmentation — already present in the base — deepens materially, preventing a coordinated fiscal or trade response.

In this context, China reassesses the credibility of US deterrence in the Indo-Pacific. The probability of a move on Taiwan — while still not the most likely outcome — shifts meaningfully leftward on the distribution. Simultaneously, the combination of energy shock, monetary policy uncertainty, and geopolitical fragmentation destabilises financial markets: bear steepening accelerates, credit spreads widen sharply, and own funds across the European insurance sector come under acute pressure.
